No Critical severity vulnerabilities were found, following is the executive summary of the High and Medium severity vulnerabilities:

It is observed that the server supports medium-strength cipher suites, including 64-bit block ciphers such as 3DES, which are susceptible to the SWEET32 attack. An attacker can exploit the weakness in 64-bit block ciphers by capturing a large volume of encrypted traffic and performing cryptographic analysis to recover portions of sensitive information, including session cookies or other confidential data. An attacker can exploit the lack of certificate trust to perform man-in-the-middle attacks by intercepting and potentially modifying encrypted communications between users and the application. It is observed that the application server supports older and deprecated TLS protocol versions such as TLS 1.0 and TLS 1.1, which may use cryptographic mechanisms that are no longer considered sufficiently secure. An attacker can exploit weaknesses in deprecated TLS versions to potentially intercept, decrypt, or manipulate sensitive communications between users and the application, depending on the supported protocol and cipher configuration.

It is recommended to disable weak 64-bit block cipher suites such as 3DES and enable only strong cipher suites such as AES-GCM or ChaCha20-Poly1305. It is recommended to install a valid SSL certificate issued by a trusted Certificate Authority and ensure that the complete certificate chain is correctly configured on the server. It is recommended to disable deprecated TLS versions such as TLS 1.0 and TLS 1.1 and configure the server to support only secure protocol versions, preferably TLS 1.2 and TLS 1.3.
